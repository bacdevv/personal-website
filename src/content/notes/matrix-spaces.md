Four fundamental subspaces explain what a matrix can produce, preserve, or send to zero.


## The main idea

$$
\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{nullity}(A)=n
$$

## Lessons

### 72. Column space of a matrix

The column space contains every combination of the columns of A. It lives in R^m for an m-by-n matrix. Its dimension is rank(A).

$$
\operatorname{Col}(A)=\{Ax:x\in\mathbb R^n\}
$$



### 73. Column space, visualized in code

Plot the columns as arrows. Two independent columns can span a plane; adding a dependent column does not enlarge that plane.

$$
\dim\operatorname{Col}(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)
$$



### 74. Row space of a matrix

The row space is generated by the rows of A and lives in R^n. Row rank equals column rank.

$$
\dim\operatorname{Row}(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)
$$



### 75. Null space and left null space of a matrix

The null space consists of inputs that A sends to zero. The left null space is the null space of A transpose.

$$
\operatorname{Null}(A)=\{x:Ax=0\}
$$



### 76. Column/left-null and row/null spaces are orthogonal

The row space is orthogonal to the null space; the column space is orthogonal to the left null space.

$$
\operatorname{Row}(A)\perp\operatorname{Null}(A)
$$



### 77. Dimensions of column/row/null spaces

Rank-nullity splits the input dimension into directions retained by A and directions sent to zero.

$$
\operatorname{rank}(A)+\dim\operatorname{Null}(A)=n
$$



### 78. Example of the four subspaces

For one rectangular matrix, compare four spaces and their ambient dimensions. The ranks of the row and column spaces must match.

$$
\dim\operatorname{Null}(A^T)=m-\operatorname{rank}(A)
$$



### 79. More on Ax=b and Ax=0

Solving Ax=b asks whether b is in the column space. Solving Ax=0 finds all invisible input directions.

$$
Ax=b\text{ is solvable}\iff b\in\operatorname{Col}(A)
$$




## Worked example — A simple column space

$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\\0&0\end{bmatrix}
$$

The second column is twice the first, so the column space is a line in R³ and rank(A)=1.


## Try it in Python

Run this with NumPy:

```python
import numpy as np
A = np.array([[1.,2.],[2.,4.],[0.,0.]])
print("rank:", np.linalg.matrix_rank(A))
print("nullity:", A.shape[1] - np.linalg.matrix_rank(A))
```
