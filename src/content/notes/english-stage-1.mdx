## Prepositions

### 25. At, on, in — place

**At** identifies a particular place or point. It does not need to mean *inside* something.

- *I saw her at the bus stop.*
- *Can I meet you at the cinema?*

**On** describes something touching a surface or line.

- *Your books are on the table.*
- *The painting looks nice on this wall.*

**In** describes something inside a space or within an area.

- *Philip's in the kitchen.*
- *Your bag's in the car.*
- *In the garden, in a park, in a town.*

<PlacePrepositions />

For towns and countries, use **in**: *in Britain*, *in Paris*.

**Important transport comparison:** We normally say **in a car**, but **on a bus** and **on a train**.

**How to decide:** Ask whether you mean a point (*at*), a surface (*on*), or an inside space or area (*in*). Some phrases, such as *at work*, are common expressions you should learn together.

### 27. At, on, in — time

The same three words also describe time. Here the difference is **a point**, **a day**, or **a longer period**.

| Preposition | Use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **at** | A point in time | *at 4 o'clock*, *at midnight*, *at lunchtime*, *at the end of the lesson* |
| **on** | A day or date | *on Monday*, *on 1st April*, *on my birthday* |
| **in** | A longer period | *in April*, *in summer*, *in 1992*, *in the morning* |

**Compare the periods:**

- *in the afternoon* → a general period of the day.
- *on Wednesday afternoon* → that period on a named day.
- *at night* → a common fixed phrase.
- *on Christmas Eve*, *on Independence Day*, *on Easter Sunday* → named days.
- *at Christmas*, *at New Year* → holiday periods.

*Example:* “I usually work **in** the mornings.” But: “I worked **on** Thursday morning.”

## Gerunds and infinitives

### 28. I like swimming — like, don't like + gerund

A **gerund** is the **-ing** form of a verb used like a noun: *swimming*, *driving*, *learning*.

- *Do you like learning English?*
- *He likes working at home.*
- *I don't like driving.*

In this lesson, use the gerund after **like**, **love**, and **hate**:

- *I love going to the cinema.*
- *She hates getting up in the morning.*

**Form:** base verb + **-ing**. For example: *go → going*, *drink → drinking*.

**Spelling rules:**

| Pattern | Example | What changes? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most verbs | *play → playing* | Add **-ing**. |
| Silent final **e** | *come → coming* | Drop **e**. |
| Short vowel + final consonant in common one-syllable verbs | *swim → swimming* | Double the last consonant. |
| Final **ie** | *lie → lying* | Change **ie** to **y**. |
| The verb **be** | *be → being* | Keep **e**. |

Do not put *a*, *an*, or *the* directly before a general activity gerund here: say **I like swimming**, not *I like the swimming* when speaking generally.

### 29. I'd like to go — would like + to-infinitive

**Would like** is a polite way to say **want**.

- *I'd like to go to the beach.*
- *Would you like to meet my boss?*

After **would like**, use **to + base verb** rather than the gerund.

| Talking about an activity | Expressing a wish politely |
| --- | --- |
| *I like swimming.* | *I'd like to swim.* |
| *Do you like cooking?* | *Would you like to cook?* |
| *She likes travelling.* | *She'd like to travel.* |

**Important difference:** *I like swimming* describes a general preference. *I'd like to swim* says what I want to do.

## Sentence structure

### 30. He always gets up early — frequency adverbs

**Frequency adverbs** tell us how often something happens: *always*, *usually*, *often*, *sometimes*, *never*.

With an ordinary Present Simple verb, put the adverb **between the subject and the verb**:

- *He **always** gets up early.*
- *The bus **usually** arrives late.*

In a negative sentence, the adverb goes **after don't or doesn't and before the main verb**:

- *I **don't often go** to the cinema.*
- *She **doesn't usually work** on Sundays.* (Additional example.)

**Sentence pattern:**

| Sentence | Order |
| --- | --- |
| Positive | Subject → frequency adverb → main verb |
| Negative | Subject → don't/doesn't → frequency adverb → main verb |

## Common mistakes

- **In Monday** → **on Monday**. Days use *on*.
- **At 1992** → **in 1992**. Years use *in*.
- **On the car** → normally **in the car**. Compare *on the bus*.
- **I like swim** → **I like swimming** for the pattern in Lesson 28.
- **I'd like going** → **I'd like to go**.
- **She often doesn't go** is possible but has a different emphasis; the Lesson 30 pattern is **She doesn't often go**.

## Short summary

- **Place:** at = point; on = surface; in = inside or area.
- **Time:** at = exact point; on = day or date; in = longer period.
- **Gerund:** use **-ing** after *like/love/hate* in these exercises.
- **Would like:** use **to + base verb**.
- **Frequency adverbs:** usually before the main verb; after *don't/doesn't* in the negative pattern taught here.
